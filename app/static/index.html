How AirSense Addresses All 6 Literature Gaps

Review of 10 IEEE/Springer air quality forecasting papers revealed critical systematic weaknesses. AirSense explicitly solves each:

Gap 1: Overfitting SOLVED

Unseen Test Failure in Literature

Literature papers frequently achieved R²=0.99 on training but collapsed to R²=0.35 on test. We enforced 5-fold cross-validation, pruned maximum tree depths (max_depth=14, min_samples_leaf=4), and transparently report both Train R² (0.970) and Test R² (0.943) with an overfit delta of only 0.027.

Gap 2: Stacking Underused SOLVED

Lack of Multi-Family Ensembles

Most studies evaluated only isolated algorithms. AirSense builds an end-to-end StackingRegressor combining Random Forest (bagging) and Gradient Boosting (boosting) with a Linear Regression meta-learner, demonstrably outperforming all individual models.

Gap 3: No Deployed Output SOLVED

Metrics Tables Without Usability

Research stopped at static tables. AirSense packages the pipeline into a real-time FastAPI service with an interactive dashboard, CPCB color categorization, and customized medical advisories for sensitive patients, outdoor workers, and the general public.

Gap 4: Non-Time-Aware Splits SOLVED

Future Data Leakage via Random Shuffling

Random train/test splits leak seasonal autocorrelation. AirSense enforces a strict chronological split (train: 2015 to Dec 2019 [19,880 samples], test: Dec 2019 to July 2020 [4,970 samples]) simulating true operational forecasting.

Gap 5: Sensor Data Quality SOLVED

Fake Zeroes & Unsystematic Imputation

Detects 3,000+ sensor dropouts (e.g. CO == 0.0) and implements an information-preserving hierarchical imputation: forward-fill within city time series, followed by City x Month seasonal median imputation.

Gap 6: Regional Specificity SOLVED

Delhi-Centric Bias in Prior Work

AirSense covers 26 Indian cities across all geographical quadrants (South, North, East, West, Northeast) and incorporates city fixed effects and historical baselines for each region.
